Bed Layout Grid

A friendly getting-started guide

This sheet is your garden's map. Before you plant a single seed, sketch where everything will go so your tomatoes don't shade out your lettuce and your zucchini doesn't swallow the whole bed.

How to use it

  1. Measure your bed and write its real width and length in the boxes at the top.
  2. Count the grid squares so each square equals a set size — for a 4×8 foot bed, each square can stand for 6 inches.
  3. Draw your plants in the squares. Tall crops (corn, pole beans, trellised peas) go on the north edge so they don't cast shade on everything else.
  4. Leave walking room. If you can't reach the middle from the edge, the bed is too wide or you need a path.
Beginner tip: Squash, melons, and cucumbers sprawl. Give them two or three squares each, even though the seed packet looks tiny. You'll thank yourself in July.
Companions to try: tomatoes + basil, carrots + onions, beans + corn. Avoid planting tomatoes where you grew potatoes last year.
