Pest & Problem “What Did That?” Worksheet

A friendly getting-started guide

Something ate your leaves overnight and you don't know what. That's okay — nobody starts out knowing. This worksheet turns mystery damage into detective notes: what you saw, when, where, and what you tried.

How to use it

  1. Describe the damage, not the culprit. Holes in leaves? Chewed stems? White fuzz? Sticky residue? Lines carved into tunnels inside leaves? Each pattern points to a different suspect.
  2. Look at the timing. Slugs and snails work at night and leave shiny trails. Caterpillars feed in daytime shade. Cutworms fell seedlings at the soil line.
  3. Sketch or describe what you find — the bug itself, egg clusters on leaf undersides, droppings, or the damage pattern.
  4. Try the gentlest fix first: hand-picking, a strong spray of water, row cover, or soapy water for aphids. Log what you did and whether it worked.
Beginner tip: Not every bug is a bad bug. Ladybugs, lacewings, bees, and ground beetles are on your side. Identify before you squish.
Common beginner mysteries: ragged holes at night = slugs or caterpillars; seedlings cut off at soil level = cutworms; curly distorted leaves = aphids; brown tunnels inside leaves = leafminers.
