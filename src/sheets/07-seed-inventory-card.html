Seed Inventory Card

A friendly getting-started guide

Seed packets multiply quietly over the winter. This card set keeps your collection honest: what you have, how old it is, and whether it's still worth planting. Cut out one card per variety — or keep them all on one sheet, whichever suits your drawer.

How to use it

  1. One card per variety. Fill in the crop, variety, source, and the year on the packet.
  2. Track the age. Most seeds stay viable 2–5 years if kept cool and dry, but onions, parsnips, and corn fade fast. The “sow by” guess helps you use old packets first.
  3. Mark location. “Shoebox in closet” counts. Future-you in February will want to know where the good seeds live.
  4. Do a paper-towel germination test for seeds older than two years: ten seeds on a damp paper towel, warm spot, count sprouters after a week. If 5 sprout, sow extra thick.
Beginner tip: Seeds want three things in storage: cool, dark, and dry. An airtight jar in the back of a closet beats a humid kitchen drawer every time.
