Harvest Weight Log

A friendly getting-started guide

There's a special kind of satisfaction in knowing your garden produced twelve pounds of tomatoes, not just “a lot of tomatoes.” This log turns harvests into numbers you can compare year to year.

How to use it

  1. One row per harvest session. Pick everything ripe, weigh or count it, write it down. A basic kitchen scale is plenty.
  2. No scale? Count instead. Log “14 cucumbers” or “2 colanders of beans.” Consistency matters more than precision.
  3. Add up the season in the totals box. Compare against last year — or against what that produce would cost at the store, if you want a smile.
  4. Note the winners. Which variety earned its space? That informs next year's bed layout.
Beginner tip: Harvest often and harvest young. Many crops (beans, zucchini, cucumbers) produce more when picked regularly. A forgotten zucchini is a doorstop; a picked one is dinner.
