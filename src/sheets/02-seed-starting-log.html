Seed Starting Log

A friendly getting-started guide

Starting seeds indoors is where the season really begins. This log keeps track of what you planted, when, and how it did — so next year you know exactly what to repeat and what to skip.

How to use it

  1. Find your frost dates first. Write your average last spring frost and first fall frost in the slots at the top. A quick web search for “frost dates” plus your zip code will find them; your local extension office knows too.
  2. Count backwards. Most seed packets say something like “start indoors 6–8 weeks before last frost.” Use the frost-date slots to figure out your actual sowing date.
  3. Log every tray. One row per variety: what you planted, the date, how many cells, and when sprouts appeared.
  4. Note the outcome. Did it sprout fast? Damp off? Get leggy? That note is gold next winter.
Beginner tip: Seeds need warmth more than light to sprout, but seedlings need serious light the moment they emerge. A sunny window is often not enough — a cheap shop light kept 2 inches above the plants works wonders.
